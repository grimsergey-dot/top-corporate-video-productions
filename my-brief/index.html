About your video

The same eight points as the template. Skip any you are not sure about — studios will ask. Tap ? for what to write.

01Goal One sentence on what should change after people watch. “Explain our platform to IT buyers so they book a demo” is a goal; “a cool video” is not.Example: Get IT managers at mid-size companies to book a demo of our backup platform.
02Audience Who watches, what they already know and what they care about. One audience per video works best.Example: IT managers at 200–2,000-employee companies; know the problem, skeptical of vendors.
03Key message The one thing viewers should remember. If you have three messages, you probably need three videos or a series.Example: Restores take minutes, not days — and nothing breaks on the way.
04Reference videos Two to six videos you like, with what you like in each: pace, humor, the way the product is shown, the music, the look. This replaces pages of adjectives.Example: Ref 1: the way the UI is animated. Ref 2: the calm, confident voice-over. Ref 3: real customers on camera.

Your saved videos above, with a line on what you like in each.

05Length & format Total length, aspect ratios (16:9, 9:16, 1:1) and cut-downs you will need for ads or social.Example: One 90-second master + 30s and 15s cut-downs, 16:9 and 9:16.
06Where it will be shown Website, LinkedIn, paid ads, sales meetings, trade shows, TV/CTV or internal. It changes the length, the opening seconds and the captions.Example: Homepage hero, LinkedIn ads, sales follow-up emails.
07Budget A range, even a rough one. Studios propose very different approaches at $10k and at $50k — without a range you get quotes you cannot compare.Example: $25k – $50k all-in.
08Deadline & must-haves The date it must be live, plus anything fixed: brand guidelines, logo animation, voice-over language, legal review, accessibility captions.Example: Live by May 12 (user conference). Brand guide attached; captions required.
