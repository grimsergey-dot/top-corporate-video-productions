Template

What to put in a video brief

The template studios actually need. The tool above fills most of it for you; the rest takes five minutes.

01

Goal

One sentence on what should change after people watch. “Explain our platform to IT buyers so they book a demo” is a goal; “a cool video” is not.

Example: Get IT managers at mid-size companies to book a demo of our backup platform.

02

Audience

Who watches, what they already know and what they care about. One audience per video works best.

Example: IT managers at 200–2,000-employee companies; know the problem, skeptical of vendors.

03

Key message

The one thing viewers should remember. If you have three messages, you probably need three videos or a series.

Example: Restores take minutes, not days — and nothing breaks on the way.

04

Reference videos

Two to six videos you like, with what you like in each: pace, humor, the way the product is shown, the music, the look. This replaces pages of adjectives.

Example: Ref 1: the way the UI is animated. Ref 2: the calm, confident voice-over. Ref 3: real customers on camera.

05

Length & format

Total length, aspect ratios (16:9, 9:16, 1:1) and cut-downs you will need for ads or social.

Example: One 90-second master + 30s and 15s cut-downs, 16:9 and 9:16.

06

Where it will be shown

Website, LinkedIn, paid ads, sales meetings, trade shows, TV/CTV or internal. It changes the length, the opening seconds and the captions.

Example: Homepage hero, LinkedIn ads, sales follow-up emails.

07

Budget

A range, even a rough one. Studios propose very different approaches at $10k and at $50k — without a range you get quotes you cannot compare.

Example: $25k – $50k all-in.

08

Deadline & must-haves

The date it must be live, plus anything fixed: brand guidelines, logo animation, voice-over language, legal review, accessibility captions.

Example: Live by May 12 (user conference). Brand guide attached; captions required.

Tips

How to write a video brief studios can quote

Show, don’t describe.

“Modern, clean, energetic” means something different to every studio. Three reference videos with one line each beat a page of adjectives — which is why this template starts with videos.

Say what you like, not just which video.

A studio cannot guess whether you liked the music, the animation style or the story structure. One line per reference removes the guesswork.

Give a budget range.

It is the single most useful line in a brief. Studios then compete on ideas inside your range instead of guessing your budget.

Keep it to one page.

A creative brief for video is a starting point for the studio’s questions, not a script. The rest is worked out in the first call.

Send the same brief to two or three studios.

Identical briefs make quotes comparable. Our rankings and price guides help you pick studios whose work and prices fit.
