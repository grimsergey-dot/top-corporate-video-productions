Why starting prices look low

A published “from” price is the cheapest package: one short video, a half-day shoot, stock music and two rounds of revisions. Most corporate projects add scripting, more shoot days, locations, talent, motion graphics and cut-downs for social, which is why most client-reported budgets land between $10,000 and $50,000.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Most US studios publish starting prices around $5k for a short corporate video (the middle half of studios start between $3.1k and $5.9k). What clients actually spend is higher: in 668 budgets reported on Clutch, 45% fell between $10,000 and $50,000 and 25% were above $50,000.

The typical published starting price for an animated explainer is $4k, with the middle half of studios starting between $2.2k and $5.5k. 50% of explainer projects reported on Clutch were under $10,000 and 42% were between $10,000 and $50,000.

Studios publish starting prices around $8k for brand films and commercials. In client-reported budgets, 51% were between $10,000 and $50,000 and 27% were above $50,000, mostly multi-day shoots with crews, talent and locations.

Among studios that publish a day rate, the median is $2k per shoot day, usually for a small crew with camera, lighting and sound. Editing, graphics and travel are billed on top.

A published “from” price is the cheapest package: one short video, a half-day shoot and limited revisions. Real projects add scripting, more shoot days, locations, talent, animation and cut-downs for social, so total budgets are usually two to five times the starting price.
