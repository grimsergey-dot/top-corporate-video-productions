What US government agencies pay for video production

Government buyers have to publish what they spend. We collected 143 single-project video contracts from 118 federal, state, city and university buyers — each with the exact amount, the vendor and what was delivered — and found the finished video for 77 of them. Advertising-agency campaign orders are listed but kept out of the figures.

Method

How we collected the data

Sources

Federal awards from USAspending.gov; state contract registers from the Washington, Oregon and Vermont open-data portals; city and county council records through Legistar and agency sites. We kept only official records — not news stories or meeting summaries.

What counts

Single-project contracts and orders for specific videos, dated November 2023 or later. We left out on-call and “not to exceed” blanket agreements, live-streaming and broadcast services, equipment, paid TV-feature programs, and contracts that were terminated before the work was done. Advertising-agency campaign orders (creative for several spots plus other assets) are shown in the table but not counted. Price per video is calculated only where the record states how many videos were delivered and the amount covers video work alone.

Amounts

The amount is the obligated or contracted total. Washington State figures are the reported contract value, which can be a ceiling rather than the final amount paid — they are marked “contract value” in the table.

Matching the videos

A video is shown with a contract only when at least two signals agree: the title in the record, a vendor credit, a release date inside the contract period, or the buyer’s own page naming the program. Every match was checked twice.

Corrections

If you were the vendor or the buyer and something is wrong, tell us and we will fix it.
