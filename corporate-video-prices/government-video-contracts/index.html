Method

How we collected the data

Sources

Federal awards from USAspending.gov; state contract registers from the Washington, Oregon and Vermont open-data portals; city and county council records through Legistar and agency sites. We kept only official records — not news stories or meeting summaries.

What counts

Single-project contracts and orders for specific videos, dated November 2023 or later. We left out on-call and “not to exceed” blanket agreements, live-streaming and broadcast services, equipment, and paid TV-feature programs where the town pays to be profiled.

Amounts

The amount is the obligated or contracted total. Washington State figures are the reported contract value, which can be a ceiling rather than the final amount paid — they are marked “contract value” in the table.

Corrections

If you were the vendor or the buyer and something is wrong, tell us and we will fix it.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

In 101 single-project contracts signed by US federal, state, city and university buyers since November 2023, the median contract was $33k. Where the contract states the number of videos, the median price per video was $19.9k, with the middle half between $5.3k and $38.9k.

Public-sector projects are different: more documentaries, PSAs and multi-video packages, procurement rules and often local vendors. We show them as a separate benchmark and do not mix them into our corporate price medians.
