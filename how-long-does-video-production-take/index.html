How long does video production really take?

Instead of studio estimates, we measured real projects: Clutch project dates for 1,206 video projects since 2021, 286 projects where a studio or its client stated the delivery time, 73 government contracts matched to the date the finished video went public, and the schedules written into 49 public RFPs.

4 weeksmedian when the delivery time is stated (286 projects)
49%of Clutch projects end the month they start or the next one
~4 monthsgovernment contract to public release (median, 73 videos)
17 weeksplanned in public RFPs for a single production
The short answer. When studios state the delivery time, a single video usually takes about 4 weeks once the brief is agreed; event recaps can ship in days. Calendar time is longer: on Clutch, half of projects run over two or more months, because approvals, scheduling and feedback rounds add weeks. Bigger budgets take longer — under $10k most projects finish within a month; $50k–$200k projects typically span about three months.

Method

How we measured

Clutch project dates

We read the video reviews on the Clutch profiles and the case-study pages of US studios; 260 of them had at least one dated video project. Each review lists the project’s start and end month. We kept single video projects that started in 2021 or later and lasted no more than 12 months, and dropped retainers, “ongoing” work and multi-year relationships. Clutch gives months, not days, so spans are counted in calendar months.

Stated delivery times

Explicit statements of time to delivery in Clutch reviews and on studios’ own case-study pages, quoted from the source. Shoot length alone (“filmed over two days”), vague phrases (“a few weeks”) and deadlines that were only required, not met, were not counted. Ranges count at the upper end.

Government contracts

Single-video contracts from official records, matched to the finished video by title, vendor credit, topic and timing. Duration is the number of days from the contract date to the video’s publication date. Series, campaigns and multi-video packages are excluded.

RFP schedules

Planned dates, milestones, review rounds and turnaround clauses copied from public RFP documents. Planned weeks are taken from the document or computed from its own start and delivery dates; one-year service terms are reported separately.

Limits

Clutch reviews are usually requested by the studio from satisfied clients, and studios publish their fastest case studies, so both sets lean toward smooth projects. Video types are assigned by keyword rules and are approximate. Publication dates can lag delivery when a buyer waits for a launch.
