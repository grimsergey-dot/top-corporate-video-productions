Methodology

How every score is built

We found studios the way buyers do — on Google and Clutch — then scored each one on the same public criteria. Open a criterion to see exactly what earns points.

Clients with revenue above $1 billion — Fortune 1000, Forbes Global 2000 or equivalent — named on the studio’s own site. Ten or more earn the maximum. Government bodies, universities and nonprofits are not counted here.

Published outcomes: sales, conversions, views, return on ad spend. Several figures earn top marks; a case-study section without numbers earns partial credit.

Clutch rating and review count, adjusted so a handful of perfect reviews cannot outrank a long record. Five reviews at 5.0 count for less than fifty at 4.9.

Years in business. Twenty years or more scores highest. If a studio does not publish its founding year, the criterion is left out rather than scored as zero.

A portfolio, case studies and client testimonials on the site — one point each.

Number of industries served (five or more scores highest) and the range of video types offered.

Published prices or ranges score highest; budget ranges in a contact form score partly. Inner pages are checked, not just the homepage.

AI production, virtual production, 3D/VFX or localization.

How many of our six source lists the studio appeared in. Kept at 5% so marketing spend alone cannot lift a studio.

Cannes Lions, Clio, Peabody or a National Emmy earn 10 points; a regional Emmy 5; Webby, Muse or Shorty 3; Telly-level awards 1 each (up to 5). Thirty points earn the full 20%. Awards dated before 2024 and directory badges do not count.

If nothing on a studio’s site is dated 2024 or later, its score is multiplied by 0.9.
