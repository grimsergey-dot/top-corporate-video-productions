Template

What to put in an animation brief

Animation studios price by style, length and how much has to be designed from scratch. These are the lines that change the quote.

01

Goal & audience

What viewers should understand or do after watching, and who they are. Animation is usually chosen to explain something complex — say what.

Example: Explain how our API connects warehouse systems, for operations leads who are not developers.

02

Style references

Two to six animated videos you like, with what you like in each: flat 2D, characters, kinetic type, isometric, 3D product renders, the color palette, the motion feel.

Example: Ref 1: flat 2D with bold color. Ref 2: smooth UI animation. Ref 3: no characters, icons only.

03

Script & voice-over

Do you have a script, or should the studio write it? Voice-over or text-only? Which language and accent?

Example: Studio writes the script from our deck; US English female voice-over.

04

Length

Animation is priced largely by the second. 60–90 seconds covers most explainers; longer usually means a series.

Example: 75 seconds + a 15-second cut-down.

05

Assets you can provide

Brand guidelines, logo files, product UI screens, 3D/CAD models, existing illustrations. Ready assets lower the cost; designing everything from scratch raises it.

Example: Brand guide, Figma UI screens, logo in SVG.

06

Where it will be shown

Website, sales calls, paid social, trade-show loop, internal training. It decides captions, aspect ratios and whether the first five seconds must work without sound.

Example: Website and sales deck; silent-autoplay version for LinkedIn.

07

Budget

A range. A 60-second 2D explainer and a 60-second 3D product film can differ several times in price; see our animated video cost guide for real figures.

Example: $15k – $25k.

08

Deadline & reviews

The live date and how many review rounds you need. Typical animation runs script → storyboard → style frames → animation, with a review at each step.

Example: Live in 8 weeks; two review rounds per stage.

Tips

How to write an animation brief studios can quote

Lead with style references.

Words like “flat”, “playful” or “premium” mean different things to different studios. Three references with one line each settle the style before anyone draws.

Say whether you need characters.

Character animation costs more than icons, type and UI. It is one of the biggest price drivers in 2D.

Separate 2D, motion graphics and 3D.

They are different crafts with different pricing. If you are not sure, save examples of each and let the studio recommend.

List the assets you already have.

UI screens, product renders and brand illustrations shorten the design stage and the bill.

Give a budget range and a date.

Animation schedules are fixed by the review stages; a realistic date and a range get you realistic proposals.
