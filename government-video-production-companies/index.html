Method

How we built the ranking

Sources

Federal contracts from USAspending.gov (video production industry codes and keyword searches, contracts only); state contract registers from Washington, Oregon, Vermont and New York; city, county, transit and university records from council packets and purchasing pages. Bid lists come from published tabulations and award recommendations. Only official records count.

What counts as an award

A contract or order whose main purpose is producing video, from 2021 to 2026. We left out equipment purchases, broadcast operations, streaming platforms and stock licenses. Federal awards performed abroad (embassies buying from local firms) are excluded, because this is a ranking of US public-sector contractors.

Who is ranked

Production companies only. Advertising agencies and IT or communications contractors that won video work are listed separately. 26 awards to individual freelancers are counted in the totals but not named.

Order

Number of awards first, then the number of different buyers (winning across agencies is a stronger signal than repeat orders from one), then total value. Value is shown but not used first, because a single multi-year support contract can outweigh dozens of small productions.

Known biases

Washington and Oregon publish every state contract in open registers, so 190 of the 242 non-federal awards come from those two states and their vendors appear more often. Federal amounts are obligated totals; Washington figures are contract values, which can be ceilings. Many cities publish only the winner, so the bidder list is a sample.

Corrections

If you are one of these companies and a contract is missing or misattributed, send us the record and we will add it in the next update.
